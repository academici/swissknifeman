# User intent and acceptance interpretation

## Core intent

The owner wants to replace repeated mechanical supervision, not remove owner authority. The
system should run a long phase through predefined slices, apply the owner's established canons to
routine conflicts, and surface a compact final result plus only the rare decisions that would
actually change those canons or cross a protected boundary.

## Operational translation

| Owner statement | Executable interpretation |
|:--|:--|
| “I usually agree with the recommendation” | Default among already legal AUTO alternatives; never authority for a new scope or contract. |
| “Set canons and boundaries at the start” | Signed run mandate references frozen authority policy and exact scope/file/validation/budget sets. |
| “Do not ask every five minutes” | Batch non-urgent OWNER decisions; routine ambiguity is resolved by lowest blast radius. |
| “Show final results and I will correct them” | Terminal packet contains accepted outputs, decisions, deviations, economics and replay evidence; corrections start a new bounded attempt. |
| “Long tasks and redefined slices” | Slice manifests are versioned. Started/accepted slices stay bound to their original hash; future pending slices may be recompiled through a recorded replan event. |
| “Avoid token overspend” | Total worker + coordinator + retry token/cost is compared against matched manual/Workflow baselines; context isolation alone is not called a saving. |

## Non-negotiable authority boundary

The supervisor may choose **within** the owner's canon. It may not infer that frequent agreement
waives security, destructive, public API, dependency, frozen-contract, external-state or package
topology gates. A worker's confidence or majority vote never promotes an action class.

## Success seen by the owner

One run should end with a short packet:

1. what was accepted and where;
2. what was auto-fixed and why it stayed inside canon;
3. any bundled owner decisions, with recommendation and blast radius;
4. remaining deviations/rollback state;
5. actual tokens/cost/time/intervention ratio against baseline;
6. a reproducible resume/replay reference.
