## 1. The closing session framed the wrong question

`phases/P5.md` P5.2 Pending Work and `handoff.md` queue a three-way choice: (a) fresh D105 grant
batch + retry, (b) accept 0/3 as terminal, (c) reopen P5.5 to sharpen the seam prompt's
"under-specified 'implement the bounded slice' wording".

All three rest on the closing finding's interpretation that the codex slices met *"already
complete, terminal, tested bytes"* and had *"nothing left to implement"*. **F4 refutes that**: at
the frozen base `bde07fb0`, slice-01's two files and slice-03's three files **do not exist in the
tree at all**. The finding described the current working tree, not the disposable worktree the
provider was given. The provider was asked to work in two nonexistent paths, with no statement of
what to build.

And option (c) as written is not executable: **F1/F3** show the description is not merely worded
badly — it is *absent from the admission object*, so no rewrite confined to
`activity.BusinessProviderActivityPort._prompt` can supply it. The frozen P5.1 catalog holds the
description (`scenario: "reconstruct bounded subset of feat(artifex): изолировать
worktree-интеграцию (P4.3)"`, plus `historical_patch_sha256`); `build-trial-admissions.py:158-181`
silently drops it when minting the admission (**F2**).

## 2. What the pilot actually proved — and what it did not

**Proved (high confidence, real live evidence):** the mechanical path is sound. Three distinct
unused D105 admissions each consumed exactly once, one fresh process each, disposable worktrees
created at exact frozen bases and rolled back byte-identically, main tree and both G2B manifests
untouched before/between/after, the `2.1.222` pin honoured and re-probed, G2B re-derived live
rather than hardcoded, and an independent validator that reported `FAIL` honestly instead of being
tuned to green. That is exactly P5.2's stated **Why** — *"prove the mechanical path is safe and
reversible"* — and it is met.

**Not proved, and not disproved:** whether the candidate can produce accepted business edits. The
task specification never reached the model. **This is a null measurement, not a negative one.**

External corroboration (`findings/P5.2-external-harness-precedent-2026-08-06.md`): across
`claude-code-action`, OpenHands, SWE-agent, the SWE-bench harness, Crystal, Uzi, claude-squad,
vibe-kanban, container-use and FleetCode, **not one dispatches a coding task as a file list with
no natural-language description** — for the benchmark harnesses it is definitional. And the
documented outcome of an under-specified prompt is precisely ours: *"a no-op/empty diff that is
still marked 'success'"*. Our two zero-diff codex `OK` records are the textbook signature of that
input class. Slice-02's `WALL_TIMEOUT` is confounded by the same gap (**F5**) and cannot on its own
indict the 180 s budget.

The same survey answers the owner's standing feasibility question: every component Artifex needs
— headless CLI worker, worktree-per-task, enforced JSON schema, tool allowlist + network-off,
runner-side wall-clock/step budgets, scope/test-based diff adjudication, fresh-process
idempotency — is shipped in existing OSS. Artifex is a superset assembly of precedented parts. The
rare part is assembling them under one auditable authority chain, which is this plan's
contribution rather than an unproven bet.

## 3. Why "accept and continue" is the expensive option

P5.3 and P5.4 consume the **same admission shape from the same P5.1 generator**: all eleven
remaining D105 rows carry the identical description-free `task` object (verified: the
`p5.4-wave-01-recovery-fixture` admission has the same twenty `task` keys, no `scenario`).

P5.4 is the economic verdict — five paired battle waves, D14/D25 floors, `GO`/`NO-GO`, and the
gate to mandatory P6. Run through a prompt that never states the task, P5.4 would predictably
produce 0/5 accepted, a `NO-GO` that keeps the plan non-terminal by design, five more one-shot
D105 rows spent, and an "economic verdict" that measures the harness's carrier gap rather than the
candidate. Continuing unchanged does not defer the cost — it spends the remaining grant budget on
a foregone conclusion before `2026-08-12T23:59:59Z`.

## 4. The three real options, with their true costs

### Option 1 — Producer-side fix via the already-pinned catalog (cheapest correct fix)

Reopen **P5.5** (the sole G2B producer, D119 precedent) so the seam resolves the task description
from the frozen P5.1 catalog: the admission already pins `task.catalog_hash`
(`df5d7545…`) and `task.task_id`, so the port can load `artifacts/P5/trial-catalog-v1.json`,
verify its hash against the admission's own pin, select the row by `task_id`, and compose a prompt
carrying `scenario` (+ `kind`, and optionally the fact that the declared files may not yet exist
at `base_commit`).

- **No admission byte changes** → all 14 `task_hash`/`admission_hash` values stay valid, D105 rows
  stay bound, `validate-trial-admissions.py`'s carrier checks stay green.
- **No frozen-contract edit** → avoids F6's "unknown field refuses before intent" cascade.
- **No new owner grant needed for P5.3/P5.4** — their admissions are untouched.
- Architecturally precedented inside the package: the port already resolves a frozen plan artifact
  by path at dispatch time (**F7**, the output-schema load).
- Cost: one P5.5 reopen cycle (adopt + positive/negative cases + re-freeze both manifests, zero
  provider processes, exactly as the D119 reopen ran), plus every downstream G2B pin moves to the
  new `generation_hash`.
- Residual: P5.2's own three admissions are spent. Option 1 alone fixes P5.3/P5.4 but leaves P5.2
  at `🟠` with a null repository-pilot result and no positive business-edit precedent.

### Option 2 — Option 1 + a fresh owner grant batch to re-run P5.2 (three new trial IDs)

Adds a new `[OWNER-GRANT]` D# authorising three replacement P5.2 trial rows (new IDs, minted by
the same P5.1 generator against the unchanged catalog, so no P5.1 freeze is rewritten — new rows
are appended under a new grant rather than reused). Then re-run the pilot through the fixed seam.

- Buys the positive business-edit precedent before P5.3's recovery pilot and P5.4's economics, so
  P5.4's `GO`/`NO-GO` measures the candidate rather than the carrier.
- Cost: one owner grant, three more live dispatches, one more P5.1-side mint cycle (append-only
  under the new grant), and the calendar risk of the `2026-08-12` D105 expiry.
- This is the only option that lets P5.4 be a *meaningful* economic verdict **and** gives P5.2 a
  real result rather than a documented null.

### Option 3 — Contract-level fix (add `task.scenario` to the admission)

Edit frozen `business-dispatch-admission-v1@1.0.0`, re-mint all 14 admissions with the new field,
re-freeze both G2B manifests, obtain a fresh grant covering the changed hashes.

- Semantically the "right" model long-term (the task spec should carry the task), and closest to
  how SWE-bench-lineage harnesses model an instance.
- Cost is the largest by far: a frozen public-contract edit, 14 changed `task_hash` values, every
  D105 binding invalidated, a new owner grant covering all remaining rows, and a P5.1 reopen —
  P5.1 is currently the plan's one cleanly terminal P5 item.
- Recommend deferring this to **P6** as a product-surface correction, recorded now, rather than
  paying it mid-pilot.

## 5. Recommendation

**Option 2** (= Option 1 + fresh three-row grant), with Option 3 recorded as a P6 follow-up.

Rationale: Option 1 is required no matter what — without it P5.3/P5.4 measure the gap, not the
candidate, and the remaining eleven one-shot rows get spent on a null. The incremental step from
Option 1 to Option 2 is one owner grant and three dispatches, and it converts P5.2 from a
documented null into the real reversible-pilot evidence P5.3 and P5.4 are specified to consume.
Option 3's semantics are better but its cost lands on the plan's only clean terminal P5 item and
buys nothing the catalog resolution does not already deliver during the pilot.

(both already forwarded in the P5.5 handoff, both outside P5.5's `Files`):

   P5.4's G3 gate.
